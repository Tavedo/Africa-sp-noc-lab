How to read the map

  • Glowing fibre — the physical subsea cable / MPLS backbone carrying every customer.
  • Green link — a customer VPN: sites that share a route-target and can reach each other.
  • Red dashes — a blocked path: different customers, kept apart by RT filtering.
  • Brass ring — Barcelona: the European hub, internet breakout and your pyATS NOC.

The one thing to say out loud in the interview

“This is a provider backbone from Barcelona into Africa. The core — the P routers — just label-switch; they never see a customer route. Each coastal PoP is a PE that drops customers into their own VRF.”

Dakar and Johannesburg are one customer with two sites. They share route-target 65100:1, so the backbone imports each into the other's VRF and they reach each other across the whole continent. Lagos is a different customer (NIGER-MPLS, RT 65100:3): its routes are never imported next door, so a Lagos→Dakar ping fails by design. Isolation here isn't a firewall — it's the RT import/export policy. That failed ping is the proof it works.

Every link on this map is asserted by the pyATS suite — same-VPN reachability must pass, cross-customer must fail.
